A plan you can actually use.

Getting a plan is easy. Checking it is true, and still having it six weeks later when you are at the airport, is the hard part.

Planning it yourself

You get a nice looking plan in one long message. Then you open ten tabs to find out if any of it is still true, and copy the bits that survive into your notes.

  • Prices from last year
  • A restaurant that shut months ago
  • Nothing keeping track of what you booked

Letting it plan

The same plan, except every price and time was looked up as it was written. It all lands in an app on your phone: your days, your hotels, your bookings.

  • Real prices for your dates, in ringgit
  • Opening times checked this week
  • Every booking saved with its reference number

Picking a hotel

It reads the hotel's own website.

Before it suggests anywhere to stay, it goes and looks the place up properly. On a trip to Desaru it came back with this.

Roof works above the restaurants and the Kids Club, 11am to 6pm, until 30 October. That covers your dates.

What it said about The Westin Desaru Coast, before anything was booked

No booking site tells you that. Neither does a score out of ten.

  • Building work and closures happening while you are there
  • What guests wrote last month, not an average of five years
  • Pick one and it redoes the whole plan around it

Before you go

It remembers what you still have to book.

The flights, each hotel, the tickets that sell out, the visa. In the order you need to do them, counting down to the day you fly.

  • Forward a confirmation email and it files itself
  • Your booking number is one tap away at the airport desk
  • Add your own, like renewing a passport
